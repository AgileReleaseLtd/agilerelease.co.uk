Questions

Before you get in touch

Anything else? Send us an enquiry or ring 020 7946 0659.

How much will it cost?

Most of our work is for a fixed fee, and our prices page lists them with VAT and the other costs. Before we start, we confirm the price in writing. If something unexpected comes up that needs more work, we tell you and agree it with you first.

Does sending an enquiry make you my solicitors?

No. We only act for you once we have checked we can (for example, that we don't already act for someone on the other side), confirmed your identity, and you have agreed our terms in our client care letter. Until then, please don't rely on anything we say as legal advice.

How quickly will you reply?

Within one working day. If you send an enquiry today, you'll hear from us by the end of the next working day.

Do I need to come into the office?

Usually not. We can meet by video or phone, and identity checks are done electronically. The exception is signing a will: in England and Wales it must be signed in front of two witnesses in person. You can do that at our office, or we can come to you.

What is a disbursement?

A cost we pay to someone else for you, such as search fees, the HM Land Registry fee or a court fee. We list the likely disbursements for each kind of work on the prices page, and pass them on at cost.

Can you act for both the buyer and the seller?

No. We act for one side only, so you always know whose interests we are looking after.

Do you do legal aid work?

No, we do not. Legal aid can be available for some family cases, for example where there has been domestic abuse. You can check on GOV.UK at gov.uk/check-legal-aid.

Is my money safe with you?

Money we hold for you goes into a separate client account, as the SRA Accounts Rules require. Our bank details will not change. Always ring us on a number you trust before sending money.

What if I'm unhappy with your service?

Tell the person handling your matter, or Niamh Quinlan, and we will try to put it right. Our complaints procedure explains the steps, and when you can go to the Legal Ombudsman.
