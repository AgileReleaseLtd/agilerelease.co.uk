    Residential conveyancing

    Buying, selling and remortgaging

    Our legal fees are fixed, by price band, for homes in England and Wales. They cover all the work in our conveyancing guide, from taking your instructions to registering the result at HM Land Registry, including the Stamp Duty Land Tax return on a purchase.

    The Solicitors Regulation Authority's Transparency Rules require firms that offer residential conveyancing to publish price information: the total cost or a range, what it includes, the likely disbursements, VAT, the key stages and timescales, and the experience of the people doing the work. This section, our probate prices and our practice area guides set that out.

    HM Land Registry fee to register a purchase

    Set by HM Land Registry and based on the price. No VAT. Registering your mortgage at the same time costs nothing extra. A new lease (for example on a new-build flat) costs more.

    Disbursements on a sale or remortgage

    Leasehold fees set by the lease

    Most leases charge fees to the landlord or managing agent. The lease sets the amount, so these are typical ranges only. On a sale, the managing agent also charges for its management pack, often £200 to £500.

    Stamp Duty Land Tax

    Stamp Duty is a tax on buying property in England, paid to HMRC. It depends on the price and your circumstances, and is not part of our fees. Our calculator gives an estimate, and HMRC has its own Stamp Duty calculator . In Wales, Land Transaction Tax applies instead.

    What is included

    • All the legal work from instruction to registration
    • Acting for your lender on a remortgage
    • Paying off one mortgage on a sale
    • The Stamp Duty Land Tax return on a purchase
    • Written reports in plain English, and calls with the person doing your work

    What is not included

    • Stamp Duty Land Tax itself
    • Defects in the title that need putting right, or indemnity insurance policies
    • A new lease, a lease extension or a deed of variation
    • Shared ownership staircasing, or paying off a Help to Buy equity loan
    • Work on a property outside England and Wales

    Key stages and timescales

    A freehold purchase usually takes 12 to 16 weeks from instruction to completion, and a leasehold flat 16 to 20 weeks. A sale takes 10 to 16 weeks, and a remortgage 4 to 6 weeks. A chain, a new-build or a slow managing agent can add time. The stages are:

    1. Taking your instructions, checking ID and funds (days 1 to 3)
    2. Receiving the contract papers and ordering searches (weeks 1 to 3)
    3. Raising questions and checking your mortgage offer (weeks 3 to 8)
    4. Reporting to you and getting the documents signed (weeks 8 to 10)
    5. Exchange of contracts and agreeing a completion date (around week 10 to 12)
    6. Completion, paying the Stamp Duty and registering you as owner (completion, then 2 to 6 weeks)

    Who does the work

      Our assumptions

      Our fees assume that:

      • the transaction is straightforward and finishes in a reasonable time
      • there are no defects in the title, and no indemnity policies are needed
      • everyone in the chain and their lawyers co-operate
      • for leasehold, the existing lease is being transferred, not a new one granted

      If something changes, we tell you before doing extra work, and confirm any new price in writing.

      Probate

      Probate and estate administration

      These prices are for estates where all the assets are in the UK and nobody is disputing the will or the estate. There are two ways we can help.

      Option 1

      Grant only

      We get the grant of probate (or letters of administration). You then collect and share out the estate yourself.

      • A meeting with a probate solicitor
      • Identifying the executors and beneficiaries
      • Working out the estate's value and the inheritance tax position for an estate where no full HMRC account is needed
      • Preparing and sending the probate application
      • Ordering two copies of the grant for you

      Option 2

      Full estate administration

      We deal with the whole estate: the grant, collecting the assets, paying debts and sharing out what is left, with estate accounts for the executors to approve.

      We charge for the time spent at our hourly rates. Most straightforward estates take 12 to 22 hours of work, which gives the range above. We tell you if it looks like going above it.

      Our assumptions

      The range above is for an estate where:

      • there is a valid will
      • there is no more than one property
      • there are no more than five bank or building society accounts
      • there are no other assets such as shares or bonds
      • there are no more than four beneficiaries
      • there are no disputes and no claims against the estate
      • no inheritance tax is payable and no full account to HMRC is needed

      What would cost more

      • Selling or transferring the property: we quote for this separately
      • Inheritance tax to pay, or a full inheritance tax account
      • Shares, other investments or a business
      • No will, a missing will or missing beneficiaries
      • Disputes between beneficiaries, or a claim against the estate

      Disbursements

      Fees we pay to others for you, at cost.

      Key stages and timescales

      A straightforward estate usually takes 6 to 12 months from start to finish.

      1. First meeting and gathering the paperwork (weeks 1 to 2)
      2. Valuing the estate and working out any inheritance tax (weeks 2 to 8)
      3. Applying for probate: GOV.UK says the grant usually arrives within 12 weeks of applying
      4. Collecting the assets (weeks 4 to 12 after the grant)
      5. Notices to creditors, with at least two months for claims, then paying debts
      6. Estate accounts and sharing out the estate
