Complaints and regulatory information

We want to get things right. If we have not, please tell us. This page explains how to complain, what we will do, and where you can go if you are still unhappy.

How to complain to us

Start with the person handling your matter. Many problems can be sorted out quickly with a phone call.

If that does not work, or you would rather speak to someone else, contact Niamh Quinlan, who deals with complaints. You can complain in any way that suits you:

  • by email to hello@quinlanmensah.example, with "Complaint" in the subject
  • by phone on 020 7946 0659
  • by letter to Niamh Quinlan, Quinlan Mensah Solicitors, 6 Halyard Walk, Chiswick, London W4

Complaining is free, and it will not change how we deal with your matter.

What we will do

  1. Within three working days we write to you to confirm we have your complaint and tell you who is looking into it.
  2. We review your file and speak to the people involved. We may suggest a call or a meeting to understand your concerns.
  3. Within eight weeks of receiving your complaint, we send our final response in writing. It explains what we found and what we will do to put things right.

If your complaint is about our bill, you may also be able to ask the court to assess it under Part III of the Solicitors Act 1974. If you do, the Legal Ombudsman may not be able to look at the bill as well.

The Legal Ombudsman

If we have not sorted out your complaint within eight weeks, or you are unhappy with our final response, you can ask the Legal Ombudsman to look at it. The Legal Ombudsman is independent, and its service is free.

Time limits

  • You must normally refer your complaint to the Legal Ombudsman within six months of our final response.
  • And it must be within one year of the problem happening, or within one year of when you found out about it.

The Ombudsman can sometimes look at a complaint outside these limits. Members of the public can complain, and so can very small businesses and some charities, clubs and trusts.

The Solicitors Regulation Authority

The Solicitors Regulation Authority can help if you are concerned about our behaviour. This could be for things like dishonesty, taking or losing your money, or treating you unfairly because of your age, a disability or another characteristic.

The SRA does not deal with complaints about the quality of our service: those go to us first, and then to the Legal Ombudsman. You can read how to raise a concern on the SRA's website .
