Funded hours in England

Help with childcare costs, in plain English

The government pays for some hours of childcare, and helps with the cost of the rest. We take all three funded schemes. Here is who can get what.

30 hours

For working parents, from 9 months

  • From the term after your child turns 9 months, until they start school.
  • 30 hours a week for 38 weeks: 1,140 hours a year.
  • You, and your partner if you have one, each expect to earn at least £2,643.68 in the next 3 months (16 hours a week at the National Living Wage, if you are 21 or over), and under £100,000 a year.
  • Apply on GOV.UK, get an 11-digit code, give it to us, and reconfirm every 3 months.

15 hours

For every 3 and 4 year old

  • From the term after your child turns 3, until they start school.
  • Every child in England, whatever you earn and whatever your circumstances.
  • 15 hours a week for 38 weeks: 570 hours a year.
  • No application to the government: just bring your child's birth certificate to us. Working parents can add 15 more hours to make 30.

15 hours

For some 2 year olds

  • Called Early Learning for 2 year olds.
  • For families who get extra support, such as Universal Credit, and for children with an Education, Health and Care plan.
  • Apply through your local council, not GOV.UK.
  • If you also qualify as working parents, you get 15 hours from each scheme: 30 in all.

Term time or stretched

Take your hours in term time, or spread them out

Funded hours are counted over the year. You can use them in the 38 school-term weeks, or stretch them across the 51 weeks we are open, with fewer funded hours each week. They cannot be squeezed into fewer than 38 weeks.

Tax-Free Childcare

£2 from the government for every £8 you pay

  • Up to £500 every 3 months for each child (£2,000 a year), or £1,000 (£4,000 a year) if your child is disabled.
  • Until the September after your child turns 11, or 16 if disabled.
  • Use it alongside funded hours, for extra hours and for food, nappies and trips.
  • The same earnings rules as the 30 hours. Reconfirm every 3 months.
  • You cannot use it at the same time as Universal Credit childcare or childcare vouchers.

Universal Credit

If you get Universal Credit

  • Claim back up to 85% of the childcare you pay for, up to a monthly limit.
  • Report what you have paid in your Universal Credit account.
  • You can still get the funded hours, but not Tax-Free Childcare at the same time.

Rules and amounts change, so check the government's Childcare Checker for your family.

Optional extras

Funded hours are free. Extras are your choice.

Your funded hours never depend on paying for anything else. Food, nappies, music and trips are optional. Say no to any of them and your child keeps their place and joins in with everything else, exactly as before.

How we bill

One clear invoice a month

  • Before a funded place starts, we fill in a short form with you that sets out your free hours, the pattern you take them in, and any extras you choose.
  • Every invoice lists funded hours at £0, then any extra hours, food, nappies and activities on separate lines.
  • Fees are worked out over the 51 weeks we are open and split into 12 equal monthly payments, with bank holidays taken off.
  • Pay by bank transfer or through your Tax-Free Childcare account, by the 1st of each month.
  • Want to change days? Tell us a month ahead. Leaving? One month's notice, by email is fine.
  • If your circumstances change and money gets tight, talk to Kwame. We would always rather find a way.
