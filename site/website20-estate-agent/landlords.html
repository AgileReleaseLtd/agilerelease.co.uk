    No. 4 The law

    A landlord's legal responsibilities

    These apply to every private let in England, whoever manages it. On our managed service we book, track and keep the paperwork for all of them. On Let only, we check them before the tenant moves in.

    • Gas safety

      Every gas appliance, pipe and flue checked by a Gas Safe registered engineer every year. Tenants get a copy of the record before they move in, and within 28 days of each check.

    • Electrical safety (EICR)

      The wiring inspected and tested by a qualified electrician at least every five years, with any urgent faults fixed. Tenants get a copy of the report.

    • Energy Performance Certificate

      A valid EPC given to tenants, rated E or better to let the home. The government has confirmed a rise to the equivalent of C by 1 October 2030, subject to Parliament.

    • Deposit protection

      The deposit protected in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receiving it, and the prescribed information given to the tenant in the same 30 days.

    • Right to Rent

      Every adult who will live in the home checked for the right to rent in England before the tenancy starts, with copies of the documents kept.

    • Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms

      A smoke alarm on every floor with living space, a carbon monoxide alarm in any room with a fixed fuel-burning appliance (not gas cookers), and all of them working on move-in day.

    • Written terms and the information sheet

      Since 1 May 2026, tenants must get the tenancy terms in writing and the government's Renters' Rights Act information sheet.

    • Repairs and a safe home

      The structure, heating, hot water, drains and wiring kept in repair, and the home free from serious hazards such as damp and mould.

    Sources: GOV.UK, Renting out your property: landlord responsibilities, Renters' Rights Act: an overview for landlords (published 1 May 2026) and Improving the energy performance of privately rented homes (government response, updated 21 January 2026). Checked on 10 November 2026.

    No. 5 What changed

    The Renters' Rights Act, in plain words

    The biggest change to renting in decades started on 1 May 2026. Here is what it means for a landlord in Bow.

    • No more fixed terms. Tenancies are assured periodic tenancies that roll on month to month. Tenants can leave with two months' notice.
    • No more section 21. To end a tenancy you need a legal reason, such as selling or moving in. You cannot use those two in the first 12 months.
    • An asking rent, and no bidding. Adverts must show the rent, and landlords and agents cannot ask for or accept offers above it.
    • One month's rent in advance at most. And nothing until the tenancy agreement is signed.
    • Rent goes up once a year. Using the section 13 notice, with at least two months' warning.
    • Pets. Tenants can ask to keep a pet, and you must consider it and give a reason if you say no.
    • No "no DSS" or "no children". It is illegal to turn people away because they have children or receive benefits.

    No. 6 For tenants

    What tenants pay, and what they don't

    Fees to tenants are limited by the Tenant Fees Act 2019, as amended by the Renters' Rights Act 2025. These are the only payments a landlord or agent in England can ask a tenant for. We do not charge tenants anything for viewings, references, contracts, inventories or check-out.

    Rent
    Up to one month in advance, and only after you have signed the tenancy agreement.
    Refundable tenancy deposit
    At most five weeks' rent, or six weeks' if the rent is £50,000 a year or more. Protected in a government-approved scheme.
    Refundable holding deposit
    At most one week's rent, to reserve a home while checks are done. Refunded, or put towards the first rent with your agreement.
    Changes you ask for
    Up to £50 if you ask to change the tenancy, for example to add a sharer, or more only if the extra cost is shown to be reasonable.
    Leaving without the right notice
    No more than the rent you would have paid if you had given proper notice.
    Bills, if the agreement says so
    Utilities, broadband, phone, council tax and the TV licence.
    Lost keys
    The reasonable cost of replacing them, with the evidence, if the agreement says so.
    Late rent
    Only once rent is at least 14 days late: up to 3% above the Bank of England base rate on the overdue amount, if the agreement says so.

    Source: GOV.UK, Fees you can charge as part of a tenancy (published 1 May 2026, updated 7 July 2026). Checked on 10 November 2026.
