Your first appointment

What to expect, and what to wear

Your first appointment takes 50 minutes. Most of it is spent working out what is going on, and the rest getting you started.

  1. Before you come

    We email you a short health form to fill in at home. Bring any scan reports, hospital letters and a list of the medicines you take.

  2. We listen

    What happened, what makes it better or worse, how it affects your days, and what you want to get back to.

  3. We look and test

    We watch how you move and test strength, movement and the nerves where needed. We explain what we are doing, and we always ask before we touch.

  4. We explain

    You get our view of what is going on, in plain words. If physio is not the right thing for you, we tell you who to see instead.

  5. We make a plan together

    Treatment usually starts the same day. You leave with exercises, sent to you by email, and a clear idea of what happens next.

What to wear

  • Knee, hip, ankle or leg Shorts, or loose trousers that roll up above the knee.
  • Shoulder, neck or back A vest top or sports top, so we can see the shoulder blades and spine.
  • Running injury Bring the shoes you run in, and wear kit you can run in for a running assessment.
  • Letters and scans Bring anything from your GP, the hospital or your surgeon.

You can bring someone with you or ask for a chaperone. If you would rather keep a layer on, tell us and we will work around it.

              Some symptoms need a doctor, not a physio

              Back pain with numbness around your bottom or genitals, or changes in your bladder or bowels. A painful, swollen calf. An injury where you heard a crack. If you have any of these, do not wait for an appointment.

                Questions

                Before you book

                Anything else? Send us a message and a physio will reply.

                Do I need a GP referral?

                No. You can book yourself in if you are paying. Some health insurers ask for a GP or specialist referral before they authorise treatment, so check when you call them.

                How many appointments will I need?

                It depends on the problem and what you want to get back to. Some people need one or two appointments and a plan to follow at home. Others, such as after surgery, come for longer. We give you our honest view at the first appointment and review it as we go. You are never tied in.

                What is the difference between physio and sports massage?

                A physiotherapist assesses and diagnoses the problem, then treats it with exercise, hands-on treatment and advice. Sports massage is hands-on work on tight or overworked muscles, for training and recovery, and does not include a diagnosis. If you have pain and do not know why, start with a physio.

                Do you see children?

                We see young people from 12 years old, with a parent or carer in the room. For younger children, we can suggest a children’s physiotherapist.

                Can I bring someone with me?

                Yes. You can also ask for a chaperone at any appointment, and you can stop or pause at any time.

                What if I need a scan or a specialist?

                We tell you, explain why, and write to your GP. If you are insured, we can suggest a specialist your insurer covers.

                Can I cancel or move my appointment?

                Yes, free of charge with 24 hours’ notice. Read the cancellation policy.

                How do I pay?

                By card or contactless after each appointment. We email you an itemised receipt you can use for an insurance or cash plan claim. Prices and insurance.
