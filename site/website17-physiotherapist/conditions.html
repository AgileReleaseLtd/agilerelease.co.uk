    Warning signs

    When to get urgent help instead

    Physiotherapy is not an emergency service. If you have any of these, do not wait for a physio appointment. This list is based on NHS advice; for the latest, see nhs.uk.

    Call 999 or go to A&E now

    Do not drive yourself. Ask someone to take you, or call 999.

    • Back pain with numbness or tingling around your genitals or bottom.
    • Back pain with changes in your bladder or bowels, such as difficulty peeing, or peeing or pooing yourself.
    • Pain, tingling, weakness or numbness in both legs.
    • Pain that started after a serious accident, such as a car crash or a bad fall.
    • Chest pain, or sudden breathlessness, especially with a painful, swollen leg.
    • An injury where you heard a crack, or the body part has changed shape, is numb, or looks blue or grey and feels cold.
    • A sudden, extremely painful headache, or a headache with weakness or numbness in the face or body, or trouble speaking, seeing or balancing.

    Get help today: NHS 111 or an urgent GP appointment

    Call 111 or use 111.nhs.uk, any time of day or night.

    • Throbbing pain and swelling in one leg, usually the calf, with red or darkened skin. It could be a blood clot, especially after an operation, a long journey or time in bed.
    • Pain with a high temperature, or feeling hot, cold, shivery or generally unwell.
    • A hot, red, swollen joint.
    • Severe pain that started suddenly, or is getting worse quickly.
    • After an injury, you cannot put weight on it or walk more than a few steps.
    • After an operation, the wound is hot, red, leaking or more painful.

    See your GP

    Not an emergency, but get it checked before physio.

    • You have lost weight without trying.
    • Pain that does not ease with rest, or is worse at night.
    • A lump or swelling, or your back has changed shape.
    • You have had cancer, and now have new back or bone pain.
    • Pain that is not getting better, or is stopping you doing day-to-day things, and you are worried.

    At the clinic. We ask about these signs at every first appointment. If one turns up, we stop and help you get the right care, and we do not charge for the appointment.

    Not sure? Call NHS 111. It is free, open 24 hours a day, and can tell you where to go.
