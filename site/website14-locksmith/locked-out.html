Locked out

What to do while you wait

Most lock-outs end with the door open and no damage within the hour. These steps keep you safe and stop you paying more than you need to.

Call 020 7946 0247

  1. Get somewhere safe and warm

    Late at night, wait somewhere lit: a neighbour's, a café, or your car with the doors locked. Save your phone battery for calls.

    If a child or someone vulnerable is alone inside and at risk, or something dangerous is left on, call 999.

  2. Check the other doors and windows

    The back door, patio doors, a side gate, a window left on the latch. Look, but don't climb or force anything: a broken window costs more than a locksmith, and a fall costs far more.

  3. Find a spare key

    A housemate, partner, family member or neighbour you've given a key to. If you rent, call your landlord or letting agent. They often keep a spare, many agents have an out-of-hours number, and your tenancy may say they must agree before a lock is changed.

  4. Call a locksmith, and get a total price

    Ask for the total with VAT, how long they'll be, and what happens if the lock can't be opened without damage. Write the price down, or ask for it by text. The questions to ask

  5. Ask the locksmith for ID

    Before they start, ask to see photo ID. We text you the name of the locksmith who's coming, and they carry a photo ID card with that name on it. If the name doesn't match, don't let them work on your door.

  6. Be ready to show you live there

Before you agree a price

Four questions to ask any locksmith

Whether you call us or someone else, these answers tell you a lot. A good locksmith answers all four straight away.

  1. "What's the total price, with VAT?" Not "from", not "depends". A total, or a clear top figure.
  2. "How long until you're here?" And will they ring if they're running late.
  3. "What's the company called, and where are you based?" A local address you can look up.
  4. "What if it can't be opened without damage?" They should tell you before drilling, and what a new lock costs.

Walk away if you hear

  • A "from £39" advert price that grows once they arrive.
  • "We'll see when we get there."
  • Drilling the lock first, before trying to open it.
  • Cash only, or a card machine in another firm's name.
  • No name, no address, or a call centre that won't say where the locksmith is.

If the work hasn't started, you can still say no. Ask for a written price before anything is done.

Burgled

If you've been burgled, do this first

It's a horrible shock. Take it one step at a time. The order matters for your safety and for your insurance claim.

  1. 999

    Happening now, or they might still be inside? Call 999.

    Don't go in. Get somewhere safe, tell the operator where you are, and wait for the police.

  2. 101

    Once they've gone, report it

    Call 101, the police non-emergency number, or report it online on the Metropolitan Police website.

  3. Don't touch anything they may have touched

    Leave doors, windows, drawers and anything moved as it is until the police say otherwise, and don't tidy up. If you must touch something to make the house safe, tell the police what.

  4. Get a crime reference number

    The police give you one when you report it. Your insurer will ask for it, so write it down somewhere safe.

  5. Call your insurer

    Most have a 24-hour claims line. Take photos of the damage, list what's missing, and keep every receipt for repairs. Ours are itemised for exactly this.

  6. Make the house secure tonight

    We can change the locks, repair a forced frame, or board up a door or window the same night. If keys were taken, change the locks: they may know your address. Price agreed first, as always.

  7. Cards, phones or laptops taken?

    Call your bank to cancel the cards. From another device, change the passwords for your email and banking first.

You don't have to deal with it alone. The charity Victim Support offers free, confidential help after a burglary, whether or not you've reported it. And if you just want someone to check the house is secure, call us.
