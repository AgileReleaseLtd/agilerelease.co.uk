The markings

What the numbers on a lock mean

Insurers and the police talk in standards. Here's what each one covers, in plain words.

BS 3621

Thief-resistant lock, key from both sides

The British Standard for locks you lock and unlock with a key from inside and out: mortice deadlocks, sashlocks and deadlocking nightlatches. They're tested against drilling, picking and forcing, with at least 1,000 different keys. Most are 5-lever, but the mark is what counts, not the number of levers.

The one most home insurance policies name for front and back doors.

BS 8621

The same, but no key needed to get out

Locked with a key from outside, opened with a thumb-turn from inside. Same security as BS 3621, and you can get out quickly in a fire. Common in flats and shared houses.

BS 10621

Two modes: home and away

Works like BS 8621 when you're in. When you go out, you set it so the inside needs a key too, so someone who breaks a window can't just open the door.

TS 007

Star ratings for cylinders and handles

For euro cylinders and the handles around them. The stars add up to three: a 3-star cylinder on its own, or a 1-star cylinder with 2-star security handles. A 3-star cylinder is tested against snapping.

What we fit as standard on UPVC and composite doors.

SS312 Diamond

Sold Secure's cylinder test

An independent test of euro cylinders against snapping, bumping, picking, drilling and pulling. Look for "SS312 Diamond" on the cylinder or its packaging.

BS EN 1303

The basic European cylinder standard

Grades a cylinder's key security and attack resistance with a code of digits. Any decent cylinder meets it, but on its own it says little about snapping. Look for TS 007 3-star or SS312 Diamond as well.

PAS 24

The whole door, not just the lock

A test of a complete door or window (frame, door and locks together) against forced entry. New homes in England have had to meet it since 2015, and many replacement doors do.

We can't tell you what your insurer accepts: that's in the "security" or "locks" part of your policy, or ask them. We'll show you the markings on anything we fit, and put them on your receipt.

Lock snapping

Why a cheap cylinder is the weak point

A euro cylinder is held in the door by one screw, through the middle, where the cam turns to work the lock. On a basic cylinder that middle is thin, and it can be snapped with simple tools.

An anti-snap cylinder has a sacrificial cut near the outside face. If it's attacked, only the front breaks off. The part that works the lock stays in the door, and the door stays locked.

Quick check: does your cylinder stick out more than about 3 mm past the handle? That makes it easier to grip. We fit cylinders that sit flush, and security handles where they're needed.
