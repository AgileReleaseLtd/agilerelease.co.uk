      Booking and cancelling

      Fair rules, written down

      Booking a term

      • Pay for the term at the start, or in three monthly payments.
      • Joining part-way through? You pay only for the sessions left.
      • Your child keeps their place each term if you rebook by the date we send you, three weeks before the end of term.
      • Bank transfer, or card at the front desk.

      Brothers and sisters

      • 10% off the fees of each brother or sister after the first, for group classes and one-to-one.
      • The child with the highest fees pays the full price; the discount comes off the others.
      • Nothing to claim: we take it off the invoice.

      Missed lessons and cancelling

      • Group classes: no refund for a missed session, but we send the worksheet, and offer a catch-up in another group that week if there is room.
      • One-to-one: cancel by WhatsApp at least 24 hours before and there is no charge. Later than that, the lesson is charged, except for the first illness each term.
      • Leaving? Tell us before half-term and you will not be charged for the next term.

      If we have to cancel

      • If a tutor is ill, another tutor covers the class, or it moves online for that week.
      • If neither is possible, the session comes off your next invoice.
      • If snow or a closure stops lessons in the centre, classes move online.

      Online lessons

      Setting up for online lessons

      Online classes and one-to-ones are live, with the same tutors, the same groups of up to six and the same reports.

      1. We send a link

        The day before the first lesson, we email a link to the lesson. It opens in a web browser, so there is nothing to install.

      2. A short test first

        For the first lesson, the tutor joins ten minutes early to check the sound and camera with you.

      3. During the lesson

        Cameras stay on. The tutor works on a shared whiteboard that your child can write on too, or they can hold work up to the camera.

      4. After the lesson

        Notes and homework arrive by email. Your child sends back a photo of their written work.

      5. Staying safe

        For children in Year 6 and below, a parent or carer stays at home and nearby. Lessons use the centre's accounts only and are not recorded.

      Safeguarding

      Keeping children safe

      A summary of our safeguarding policy, which follows the Department for Education's guidance for out-of-school settings. Ask us for the full policy at any time.

      • Every tutor and member of staff has an enhanced DBS check before they start (an example claim on this sample site).
      • Everyone who works here does safeguarding training every year, and the safeguarding leads more often.
      • Pupils sign in and out. Children in Year 6 and below are collected by an adult you have named; older pupils can walk home with your written agreement.
      • There are always at least two adults in the building while children are here.
      • Tutors never contact pupils on personal phones or social media. Messages go to parents, or to sixth formers through the centre's account with a parent copied in.
      • Online lessons use the centre's accounts only and are not recorded. We do not take photos of pupils.
      • Any worry about a child goes to the safeguarding lead the same day, and is written down.

      Questions

      Questions parents ask

      Anything else? Message us and we will reply the same working day.

      How big are the groups?

      Six pupils at most, and five for A level. Most groups have four or five, so the tutor can check every child's work in every lesson.

      My child is behind, or ahead. Will a group suit them?

      The baseline assessment in the first two weeks shows where they are. Groups are set by school year and, for GCSE maths and science, by tier. If a group is not the right fit, we will say so and suggest one-to-one instead.

      Do you follow my child's exam board?

      Yes. Tell us the board and the specification, and we teach to it: AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR or WJEC Eduqas. If you do not know, ask the school or look at the front of an exercise book or a mock paper.

      Is there homework?

      Yes, a little and often: about 20 minutes a week at Key Stage 2, and up to 45 minutes for GCSE and A level. It is marked by the next lesson.

      Do you help children with dyslexia or other additional needs?

      Our tutors are teachers, not specialist assessors. Tell us what helps your child, such as shorter tasks, coloured paper or extra time, and we will adapt. If a specialist would serve your child better, we will tell you honestly.

      Can my child switch between the centre and online?

      For one-to-one lessons, yes, any week with 24 hours' notice. Online group classes are separate groups, so moving between them depends on places.

      Can I stay during the lesson?

      There is a small waiting area by the door. Parents do not sit in group lessons, so pupils can relax, but the tutor always talks to you after the free trial.

      What happens in the school holidays?

      There are no group classes in half-term or the holidays. One-to-one lessons can carry on if you ask. We run Easter revision mornings for Year 11 and Year 13, and tell current families the dates each spring.
