11+

A careful word on the 11+

Three things to find out first:

  1. Which schools you will apply to.
  2. What each school's test covers.
  3. When registration closes.

Not sure? Bring the list to your free trial and we will go through it with you.

The 11+ is not one national exam. Each grammar school, or group of schools in an area, decides what its test covers, who writes it and how places are given. Families near us apply to schools in Bexley, Bromley and Kent, and to independent schools, and each has its own rules.

  • Tests can include English, maths, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning, and some ask for a piece of writing.
  • Most grammar school tests are sat early in Year 6, often in September, after registering in Year 5. Independent schools set their own tests, often later in Year 6.
  • Passing a test does not always mean a place: many schools have more children who pass than places to offer.
  • Dates and formats can change from one year to the next. Always check the school's own website or your council's admissions team.

We match our 11+ classes to the tests of the schools you are applying to, and we will tell you honestly how your child is doing. We do not promise places.

Progress

How we track progress

You see where your child started, what has improved and what comes next. No guesswork, and no surprises at parents' evening.

  1. 1

    Baseline assessment

    In the first two weeks: a short written check matched to your child's year and exam board, and a chat about what feels hard. You get the results by topic.

  2. 2

    Every lesson

    A five-minute recap quiz, new work in small steps, and homework of 20 to 45 minutes depending on age, marked by the next lesson.

  3. 3

    Termly report

    One page by email in the last week of term: attendance, homework, each topic marked secure, getting there or not yet, and next steps.

  4. 4

    Parent meetings

    Fifteen minutes with the tutor each term, in the centre or by video, and any time you are worried.

Sharing real results, honestly

When a centre publishes results, it should say how many pupils they cover, over what time, and how they were measured. For example:

“Of the number Year 11 pupils who had GCSE maths with us for two terms or more in year, number improved by two grades or more from their baseline.”

The blanks stay blank on this sample. A real centre fills them in from its own records.
