Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

MTD, in plain English

Since 6 April 2026, many sole traders and landlords keep digital records and send HMRC a short update every quarter, as well as the yearly tax return. Here is who has to join, when, and what it means for you.

Who has to join, and when

It depends on your qualifying income

MTD for Income Tax is for sole traders and landlords registered for Self Assessment. You join if your qualifying income is more than the threshold for the tax year HMRC looks at.

  1. 6 April 2026

    £50,000

    Qualifying income over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025

  2. 6 April 2027

    £30,000

    Qualifying income over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026

  3. 6 April 2028

    £20,000

    Qualifying income over £20,000 in 2026 to 2027

What counts as qualifying income

  • Your income from self-employment and from property, added together.
  • Before any expenses: it is your turnover and rent, not your profit.
  • UK and foreign property both count if you were UK resident.
  • Own a property jointly? Only your share of the rent counts.

What does not count

  • Wages from a job taxed through PAYE.
  • Dividends, including from your own company.
  • Your share of profit as a partner in a partnership.
  • The State Pension and private pensions.

Good to know

  • HMRC writes to people who need to join, but it is your job to check.
  • Some people are exempt, for example if you are digitally excluded. Some exemptions must be applied for.
  • Partnerships will join later; no date is set yet. Limited companies are not part of MTD for Income Tax.
  • Once in, you can choose to leave if your qualifying income stays under the threshold for three tax years in a row.

What you do in MTD

Four steps a year, the same every year

The tax you pay and the dates you pay it do not change. What changes is how often HMRC hears from you.

  1. 365 days of records

    Keep digital records

    Record your self-employment and property income and expenses in HMRC-recognised software as you go, or in a spreadsheet linked to bridging software.

  2. 4 updates a year

    Send quarterly updates

    Totals of your income and expenses for the tax year so far, sent from your software. Updates are summaries, not tax returns, and no tax is paid with them.

  3. 1 tax return a year

    Send your tax return by 31 January

    After the tax year, through your MTD software. You add other income, such as savings interest or dividends, and any adjustments. You may hear it called the final declaration.

  4. 2 payment dates, as now

    Pay as you do now

    Payment dates stay the same: 31 January, and 31 July if you make payments on account.

Quarterly update deadlines, 2026 to 2027

Standard periods run from 6 April and each update covers the year so far. If your accounts run to 31 March, you can pick calendar quarters in your software before your first update.

Penalties

A gentle first year, then points

Late updates and returns earn penalty points, like a driving licence. Points only turn into a fine once you reach the limit.

  • 0

    Points for late updates in 2026 to 2027

    No penalty points for a late quarterly update in the first year. You still need to send all four before your tax return.

  • 4 = £200

    From 2027 to 2028

    One point for each missed update or tax return deadline. At 4 points you pay £200, and another £200 for each deadline missed after that.

  • 24

    Months until a point expires

    Below 4 points, each point is removed 24 months after the deadline you missed.

Paying late is separate: penalties start after 15 days (30 days in your first year under the new rules), and HMRC charges interest from the day after the payment was due.

Software

You need HMRC-recognised software

Your records, your quarterly updates and your tax return all go through software that works with MTD for Income Tax. HMRC publishes a list of recognised products, and a finder that asks a few questions to narrow it down. HMRC does not recommend any one product.

  • All-in-one software keeps your records and sends everything to HMRC.
  • Bridging software sends updates from the spreadsheet you already keep.
  • Free products exist for simple tax affairs, though some limit what you can do, such as the number of transactions.
