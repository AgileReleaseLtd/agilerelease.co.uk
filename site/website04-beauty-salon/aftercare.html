Aftercare

Looking after it at home

What to do, and what to avoid, in the days after your treatment. This is general advice. If something does not feel right, message us: we would much rather hear about it early.

Lash extensions Gel and BIAB Waxing Lifts, tints and brows

If you have a serious reaction

If your eyes, face, lips or tongue swell, or you find it hard to breathe, call 999 straight away. For a reaction that is uncomfortable but not an emergency, speak to a pharmacist or call NHS 111, then let us know.

Lash extensions

With good care, extensions look their best for two to three weeks, then need an infill. Losing a few each day is normal: your natural lashes shed, and the extensions go with them.

First 24 hours

  • Keep them dry while the glue finishes setting: no face washing with water, steam, saunas or swimming.
  • Keep your face out of the shower spray, and skip very hot, steamy showers.
  • Try not to touch or rub them.

Every day after that

  • Wash them every day with an oil-free lash cleanser and a soft brush, rinse, and pat dry. Clean lashes last longer and keep your eyelids healthy.
  • Brush them through gently with a clean spoolie, starting halfway down the lashes.
  • Sleep on your back if you can, or on a silk pillowcase.

Please avoid

  • Oil-based make-up removers, eye creams and serums on the lash line: oil softens the glue.
  • Mascara, especially waterproof, and lash curlers.
  • Picking or pulling extensions off. They take your own lashes with them. Book a removal instead.

Message us if

  • Your eyes or eyelids are red, itchy, swollen or sore, or keep watering after the first day.
  • A lash points into your eye or feels scratchy.
  • You think you are reacting to them. We can remove them safely, usually the same or next day. Please do not try it at home.

If your eye is painful or your sight changes, see a pharmacist, an optician or your GP, or call NHS 111.

Gel and BIAB nails

Gel polish lasts about two weeks on hands, and BIAB three to four. Most problems start with lifting at the edges, usually from water, knocks or picking.

First 24 hours

  • Your nails are fully set when you leave, so you can carry on as normal.
  • Avoid long hot baths, saunas and hot tubs for the first day: heat and soaking can make the edges lift.

Every day

  • Rub cuticle oil into the skin around each nail once or twice a day. It keeps nails flexible and helps stop lifting and chips.
  • Wear gloves for washing up, cleaning and gardening.
  • If a nail needs shortening, file it gently rather than clipping it.

Please avoid

  • Peeling, picking or biting it off. It takes layers of your own nail with it and leaves them thin and sore.
  • Using your nails as tools, to open cans or scrape off labels.
  • Gluing a lifted edge back down: water gets trapped underneath.
  • Acetone and strong solvents, which soften the gel.

Message us if

  • A nail lifts, chips or breaks. Come in and we will put it right.
  • You get itching, redness, a rash or swelling around your nails or fingertips, even weeks later. It can be an allergy to the products, and carrying on can make it worse, so tell us before your next appointment.

If the skin around a nail is hot, swollen, painful or weeping, or you see green or dark marks under the product, it may be infected: see a pharmacist or your GP.

Book an infill or a removal at three to four weeks. We can soak it off safely in 20 minutes.

Waxing

Some redness and small bumps are normal after waxing, and usually settle within a day or two. These steps help your skin settle and keep ingrown hairs away.

First 48 hours

  • Avoid hot baths, saunas, steam rooms, pools and hot tubs.
  • No sunbathing, sunbeds or fake tan on the waxed skin.
  • Skip the gym and tight clothes. After an intimate wax, loose cotton underwear helps.
  • Keep perfumed products, and deodorant after an underarm wax, off the area.

After that

  • Keep the area clean, and touch it as little as you can.
  • From the third day, exfoliate gently two or three times a week and moisturise daily. It is the best way to prevent ingrown hairs.

Please avoid

  • Shaving between waxes. It restarts the hair cycle and makes the next wax harder.
  • Squeezing spots or digging at ingrown hairs.
  • Booking a wax on skin treated with retinoid creams in the last week, or while taking isotretinoin (and for six months after). Tell us about any skin medication.

Message us if

  • Redness, swelling or bumps last longer than three days.
  • Any skin was lifted or broken during the wax and is not healing.

If the skin is weeping, hot or more and more painful, or the redness is spreading, it may be infected: see a pharmacist or your GP.

Your next wax is best in four to six weeks, when the hair is about 5 mm long.

Lash lifts, tints and brows

A lash lift lasts six to eight weeks, brow lamination about six, and tints four to six. The first day matters most.

First 24 hours

  • Keep lashes and brows dry: no water, steam, saunas or swimming.
  • No mascara or brow make-up, and try not to rub them.
  • Sleep on your back if you can, so a fresh lift is not pressed flat.

After that

  • Brush laminated brows into place each morning with a clean spoolie.
  • Be gentle with oil-based removers on tinted lashes and brows: they fade tint faster.

Message us if

  • You get itching, stinging, redness or swelling around your eyes or brows, during or after a tint. Rinse gently with cool water and contact us.
  • Something gets into your eye and stays sore after rinsing: see a pharmacist or an optician.
