Guide How we work

How a job runs, start to finish

Building work comes with paperwork. Here is what happens and in what order, what planning, permitted development, party walls and building control mean, and how you pay.

02 The six steps

What we do, and what you do

  1. 01

    First visit and quote

    Free · about 2 weeks

    We come to see you, usually within a week of your message. Ray looks at the structure, the drains and the access, and Nina measures up. We talk about what you want and what you want to spend.

    Within ten working days you get a written quote: itemised, with what's included, what isn't, and an allowance for anything we can't price until we open up.

    Your part: send a few photos beforehand if you can, and set your budget with about 10% spare for surprises.

  2. 02

    Design and drawings

    From £2,200 · 3 to 4 weeks

    Nina draws the house as it is and as it will be: plans, elevations and a section. Change the layout as often as you need before we fix it. A structural engineer then works out the beams and the foundations.

    Already have an architect? We build from their drawings instead.

    Your part: tell us early about the things that matter to you, like the light, storage, or where the kitchen sink goes.

  3. 03

    Planning and building control

    About 8 weeks

    We apply for whatever your project needs, explained below: planning permission or a lawful development certificate, building control approval, party wall notices, and a build-over agreement if there's a sewer in the way. They run side by side.

    Your part: have a friendly word with the neighbours before the notices arrive. It makes everything easier.

  4. 04

    The build

    8 to 16 weeks for most jobs

    We agree a start date and a week-by-week plan. One of us is on site every day, and you get a WhatsApp update with photos every Friday. Building control inspects at set stages: foundations, drains, steels, insulation and the finish.

    Your part: make choices on time (tiles, taps, door handles), so nothing waits for a decision.

  5. 05

    Sign-off

    The last week

    Building control's final inspection and completion certificate, the electrical certificate, and a gas safety certificate if there was gas work. Then we walk round with you, write a snag list, and put each item right.

    Your part: keep the certificates safe. Your buyer's solicitor will ask for them when you sell.

  6. 06

    Guarantee

    10 years structural · 2 years the rest

    Our written guarantee covers structural work for ten years and everything else for two. We come back after six months to fill the small cracks that appear as new plaster dries and timber settles.

    Your part: tell us about anything that isn't right, and we'll arrange a visit.

03 Permissions explained

Planning, party walls and building control in plain English

For houses in England. It's a general guide, not legal advice.

Planning permission

Permission from your council to build something. You apply with drawings, the council asks the neighbours, and it usually decides a householder application within eight weeks.

When it applies: most two-storey extensions, anything bigger than permitted development allows, mansards and front dormers, and any extension to a flat or maisonette. A listed building also needs listed building consent.

Permitted development

A national allowance that lets you extend a house without applying for planning permission, within set limits.

For example: a single-storey rear extension up to 3 m deep on a terraced or semi-detached house (4 m if detached), up to 4 m high. Or a loft that adds up to 40 m³ to a terraced house (50 m³ for others), with no dormer on the roof slope that faces the road. The rules are tighter in conservation areas, some councils remove them with an "Article 4 direction", and flats and maisonettes don't have them.

Prior approval

A route for longer single-storey rear extensions: up to 6 m deep on a terraced or semi-detached house, 8 m if detached. You tell the council, the neighbours can comment, and the council has 42 days to decide.

When it applies: when you want to go deeper than the usual permitted development limit, but stay within the other rules.

Lawful development certificate

The council confirms in writing that your project is permitted development. It's optional, but we always recommend one: buyers' solicitors ask for it when you sell.

When it applies: any project that relies on permitted development, such as most rear dormer lofts.

Building regulations and building control

Rules for how the work is built: structure, fire safety, insulation, drainage, ventilation and stairs. Building control is the check that the rules are met. The council's team or a registered building control approver inspects at set stages and issues a completion certificate at the end.

When it applies: almost every extension and loft conversion, even when planning permission isn't needed. Also removing a load-bearing wall, new drainage and replacement windows. Electrical and gas work is certified by registered trades.

Party wall agreements

The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 means you must give your neighbours written notice before certain work: at least two months for work to a shared wall, one month for building on the boundary or digging near their foundations. If they agree in writing, the work can go ahead. If not, surveyors agree a "party wall award" setting out how it is done, usually at the cost of the person building.

When it applies: cutting into a shared wall (for example to rest the beams for a loft), building a wall on the boundary, or digging foundations within 3 m of a neighbour's building and deeper than theirs. It's separate from planning.

Build-over agreement

In London many houses have a shared sewer running behind them. Before building over or close to a public sewer, you need the water company's agreement, which is Thames Water across most of London.

When it applies: usually when the new work is within 3 m of a public sewer. We check the sewer maps at the first visit.

04 Payments

No big deposit. Stage payments, tied to work you can see.

Here is how a typical extension is paid for. Lofts, kitchens and bathrooms follow the same idea with fewer stages.

    • No deposit to book your start date.
    • Each invoice follows a finished stage that you've seen. Seven days to pay, by bank transfer.
    • Made-to-order items, such as steels and glazing, are invoiced at cost when ordered, with the supplier's invoice.
    • Extra work only with a written price you've agreed first.
    • You hold back the last 5% for three months, until any snags are put right.
    • We never ask for cash, and never ask you to pay for work ahead of time.

    05 Insurance and guarantee

    Covered while we build, guaranteed after

    Public liability

    £5 million of cover for damage to your home or a neighbour's, and injury to anyone outside our team.

    Employer's liability

    The cover the law requires for a firm with staff, so our team is insured while working at your house.

    Contract works

    Covers the new work against fire, flood and theft until we hand it over. Tell your home insurer before we start as well: some policies need to know.

    Our guarantee

    In writing: ten years on structural work, two years on everything else, and a return visit after six months.
