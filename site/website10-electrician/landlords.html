What the law asks of you

The Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, in short:

  1. Have the electrics checked at least every five years

    By a qualified and competent person. The report gives the date of the next check, which can be sooner than five years.

  2. Give your tenants a copy of the report

    Existing tenants within 28 days of the inspection, new tenants before they move in, and anyone hoping to rent within 28 days of asking.

  3. Fix what the report says must be fixed

    If it lists anything coded C1, C2 or FI, the repair or further investigation must be done within 28 days, or sooner if the report says so.

  4. Confirm the work in writing

    Give your tenants and the council written confirmation that the work is done, within 28 days of finishing it.

  5. Send the council a copy if it asks

    Within seven days of a written request. Keep a copy to give to whoever does the next inspection.

Councils can fine landlords up to £30,000 for breaking these rules. This is a short summary, not legal advice: read the government's guide for landlords.

The codes on a report

C1

Danger present. We make it safe before we leave, with your agreement.

C2

Potentially dangerous. Needs fixing soon: within 28 days for a rented home.

FI

Further investigation needed. Something we couldn't fully check in the time, such as a circuit we couldn't trace.

C3

Improvement recommended. Advice, not a fail.

Any C1, C2 or FI makes the report unsatisfactory. We quote for the repairs separately, and you choose who does them.
