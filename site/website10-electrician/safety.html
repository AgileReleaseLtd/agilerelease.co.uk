Know your fuse box

Find it before you need it: often under the stairs, in a hall cupboard or by the front door. A modern one (a consumer unit) looks something like this.

  1. 1
    Main switch

    Turns off the electricity to the whole home. Use it in an emergency.

  2. 2
    RCD (has a T or Test button)

    Cuts the power in a fraction of a second if electricity leaks to earth, for example through a person. Press Test every six months: it should switch off. Then switch it back on.

  3. 3
    Circuit breakers

    One for each circuit: lights, sockets, cooker. Each switches off if its circuit is overloaded or has a fault.

  4. 4
    Labels

    Which switch does what. No labels? We'll add them next time we visit.

Fuse wire in a holder instead of switches means an old fuse box. Never replace fuse wire with anything thicker, or with anything that isn't fuse wire.

If your fuse box keeps tripping

A trip is the fuse box doing its job: something on that circuit isn't right. Most of the time it's an appliance, and you can find it yourself.

Which switch has gone down?

One circuit breaker has tripped

  1. Switch off or unplug everything on that circuit. For a lighting circuit, switch the lights off.
  2. Push the breaker fully down, then back up to on.
  3. Turn things back on one at a time. If it trips when you plug something in, that appliance is probably faulty: leave it unplugged.
  4. If it trips with nothing plugged in, or straight away, leave it off and call us.

Trips when a heater, kettle and washing machine all run at once? The circuit may be overloaded. Spread things out and tell us: it may need another circuit.

An RCD has tripped

  1. An RCD covers several circuits, so first unplug everything on them, and switch off outside lights.
  2. Switch the RCD back on. If a breaker under it has also gone down, switch that on too.
  3. Plug things back in one at a time, waiting a minute between each. Kettles, washing machines, dishwashers and outside lights are common causes.
  4. If it trips with everything unplugged, the fault is in the wiring. Leave it off and call us.

Trips when it rains? An outside light or socket may have water in it. Leave it off until it's checked.

The main switch is off

  1. The main switch rarely trips by itself. Someone may have switched it off, or your board may have a main RCD (with a T button) that tripped.
  2. Look for anything obvious: water near sockets or lights, a burning smell, scorch marks. If you find any, leave it off and call us.
  3. Otherwise switch it back on. If it goes off again, leave it off and call us.
  4. Main switch on but the whole home dark? Check whether your neighbours have power, and your credit if you have a prepayment meter. If the street is off, call 105.
  • Never hold a switch up or tape it on. It's switching off to protect you.
  • Don't keep resetting it. If it trips twice with the cause unplugged, leave it off and call us.
  • Keep the cover on. There's nothing inside a fuse box you need to touch.

When something looks or smells wrong

Plain do's and don'ts. When in doubt, switch off and call.

Exposed wires or damaged sockets

  • Never touch exposed wires, even if you think the power is off.
  • Keep children and pets away.
  • If you can reach it safely, switch off at the main switch with dry hands. Don't touch a fuse box that is wet or damaged.
  • Don't use a cracked, loose or scorched socket, or anything with a damaged cable.
  • Call us. Don't tape it up and carry on.

Burning smell, buzzing or scorch marks

  • Switch off that circuit at the fuse box, or the main switch if you're not sure which one.
  • Unplug anything nearby, if it's safe to.
  • Smoke or flames: get out, close the door behind you and call 999.
  • Never use water on an electrical fire.
  • Have it checked before you use it again.

If someone gets an electric shock

  • Don't touch them if they may still be in contact with the electricity.
  • Switch off at the main switch. If you can't, push the source away with something dry that doesn't conduct, like a wooden broom handle, while standing on something dry.
  • Call 999. Even a small shock can affect the heart, so they should be checked over.

Water and electrics

  • Water coming through a light or onto sockets? Switch off at the main switch, if you can reach it without touching water.
  • Don't touch switches with wet hands, or stand in water to reach the fuse box.
  • Stop the leak at the stopcock and let everything dry out.
  • Have the circuits tested before you switch them back on.

Power cuts

  • Check whether your neighbours or the street lights are off too.
  • If they are, it's the network: call 105, free, for updates. In most of London the network is run by UK Power Networks.
  • If it's just you, check your fuse box for a tripped switch, and your meter credit if you're on prepayment.
  • Switch off heaters, irons and anything else that shouldn't start up by itself when the power comes back.

Extension leads and adaptors

  • Never plug one extension lead into another.
  • Plug heaters and other power-hungry appliances straight into a wall socket.
  • A 13 amp lead shares 13 amps: a kettle, a heater and a toaster together are too much.
  • Unwind cable reels fully before use. Coiled up, they can overheat.

Smoke and heat alarms

  • Press the test button once a month.
  • Replace alarms every ten years, or sooner if the maker says so.
  • Use a heat alarm in the kitchen, not a smoke alarm, so cooking doesn't set it off.

Doing it yourself

  • Small like-for-like swaps, such as a socket faceplate or a light fitting, are allowed in England. Switch off at the fuse box first: a light switch alone isn't enough.
  • A new circuit, a new fuse box, or work in a bathroom must be notified to building control. Leave those to a registered electrician who can certify them.
